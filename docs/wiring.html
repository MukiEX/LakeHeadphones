Bench sheet · three builds

LakeHP 5.1 wiring

The ESP32-S3 is the USB 5.1 sound card and runs the virtual rooms. It streams stereo over I2S to a classic ESP32, which handles Bluetooth to your headset. It takes four wires, plus an optional fifth for power. Further down: the Pico 2 W single-board build, and the ESP32-S3 + nRF5340 LE Audio build.

Boards are drawn schematically. Find the pins by their silkscreen labels. Exact header positions differ between DevKit revisions and clones.

Before you power it up

Which S3 port is which

Plug the PC into the S3's USB port; that is the 5.1 sound card. Flash and read logs through the UART port. Both can be connected at once.

Only one 5V source

Connect the 5V wire only if the ESP32's own micro-USB is unplugged. Otherwise two USB supplies end up tied together. While you are flashing the ESP32, leave the 5V wire off.

Keep it short

BCLK runs at 3 MHz. Jumper wires under about 15 cm are fine. On longer runs, twist each signal with a ground wire or add a 33 Ω series resistor at the S3 end.

Different pins

Every pin can be changed in idf.py menuconfig on both boards. On the S3 avoid GPIO19/20 (USB), 0 (BOOT), 43/44 (UART) and 26–37 (flash/PSRAM). On the ESP32 avoid GPIO6–11.

Buttons on the S3

Tap BOOT to cycle Small, Medium and Large rooms. Hold it for 2 s to toggle low-latency stereo: a plain downmix with no room, about 40 ms less buffering. Two high beeps mean LL is on.

Left and right reversed?

Turn on LHP_SWAP_LR in the ESP32 transmitter's menuconfig.

Single-board build

Pico 2 W: nothing to wire

USB and Bluetooth are both on the board. BOOTSEL is the button at runtime: a single press cycles the rooms, a double press toggles low-latency stereo, and holding it for 2 s re-pairs. Holding BOOTSEL while you plug in still enters the UF2 bootloader as usual.

LE Audio build

ESP32-S3 + nRF5340 DK

Use this one for LE Audio headsets such as the Sony WF-1000XM5 or WH-1000XM6. The S3 is the same USB 5.1 card running the same rooms. The nRF5340 DK runs Nordic's nrf_audio unicast client, encodes LC3 and talks to the headset. Here the nRF is the I2S clock master and the S3 follows its clock, so the clock wires point the other way from the ESP32 build.

P1.07, P1.05 and P1.09 are on the DK's P1 header. On the S3, flash with sdkconfig.defaults.nrf5340 so it runs I2S as a 16-bit slave.

Leave the DK at 3 V

Keep the DK's VDD switch on its default 3 V setting (not 1.8 V). The S3's 3.3 V I/O and the nRF's 3 V I/O read each other fine at that level.

Check the headset first

The headset needs LE Audio turned on in its own app, and it must accept a unicast connection from a non-phone source. Set its name in overlay-lakehp.conf.

Latency

Rooms: about 35 ms in the S3 plus about 40–60 ms over the LE Audio link. Low-latency stereo cuts the S3 part to under 20 ms.
